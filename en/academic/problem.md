# Problem & Problem Set Standards

The question bank is the core of LiuMing. All questions in the question bank are contributed by community users and published after administrator review; problem sets organize questions by theme, and public problem sets also require administrator review. The quality of questions directly determines the effectiveness of practice and learning for all students across the site, so contributing questions and problem sets is something that must be taken seriously.

This page defines the standards to follow when contributing questions and problem sets. Behavior that violates the standards may be rejected or reported, and in serious cases will incur penalties such as warnings, mutes, or bans in accordance with the [Community Rules and Penalties](/en/policies/rule).

## 1. Originality Requirements

**Questions must be original, or adapted with explicit authorization.** This is the foundation of the question bank's credibility.

### No Plagiarism

- **No copying**: do not copy questions from other platforms, teaching materials, teacher courseware, or any other source and submit them as your own contribution. Copying includes both wholesale reproduction and "just change a few numbers" style reposting.
- **Adapt with caution**: when adapting someone else's question (modifying data, changing the context, altering the question asked), if the core structure and tested points remain substantially the same as the original, it should be treated as a citation rather than an original, and must be cited with the source and authorized (if copyrighted content is involved).
- **Translation is also adaptation**: translating a question from another language into Chinese and submitting it without noting the original source likewise constitutes infringing reposting.

### Cite Sources When Referencing

If a question does draw on someone else's ideas, data, materials, or question framework, you should **note the source** in the solution or question description. The source should be as specific as possible:

- Textbooks and teaching materials: note the book title, edition, page number, or unit.
- Past exam papers and competitions: note the year, region, and event name.
- Online resources: note the source link and the author.

Attribution is not "showing weakness"; it makes a question traceable to its source, makes it easy for readers to verify, and protects the rights of original creators.

### Copyright Red Line

Do not upload copyrighted, unauthorized textbook originals, real exam papers, or paid materials. Note the following:

- The same rule applies to full paper library uploads (see the [Full Paper Library Guide](/en/basic/paper-uploads)).
- "You can find it online" is not the same as "you can freely repost it." Authorization is a precondition for reposting, not a general default.
- When in doubt, it is better to cite the source and explain the adaptation than to pretend the work is original.

::: danger Consequences of Plagiarism
Plagiarizing others' questions or uploading infringing content is a serious violation. Once confirmed, at a minimum the question will be rejected and a **warning** issued; in serious cases you may face **mutes or account bans**, and it will be counted into the [violation period](/en/policies/rule). Original authors whose work has been plagiarized can complain through the report channel.
:::

## 2. Question Statement Quality Standards

A qualified question should first of all be **clear, rigorous, and independently completable**. During review, administrators focus on the following aspects:

### Clear Expression

- **Unambiguous meaning**: the statement should fully specify the conditions, objects, and what is being asked, without relying on implicit premises outside the statement (unless they are accepted teaching conventions, such as "within the elementary school scope").
- **Standard language**: use standard subject terminology and avoid colloquialisms, incomplete sentences, or self-invented symbols. For Chinese questions, pay attention to punctuation and wording; for English questions, pay attention to spelling and capitalization.
- **Self-consistent conditions**: the conditions given must not contradict one another and must be sufficient to support a unique (or the required) answer. When data is involved, check whether the values have been verified (for example, whether three sides in a geometry question truly form a triangle, or whether the numbers in a word problem actually work out).

### Formulas and Typesetting

- **Typeset formulas with LaTeX**: formulas and symbols in STEM questions (mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, etc.) should be entered using the formula input rather than written as plain text or images. The editor supports **visual formula input**, which lets you compose LaTeX graphically with real-time preview without memorizing syntax (see the [Input & Editor Guide](/en/basic/editor)).
- **Neat formatting**: make good use of the editor to structure the question statement (numbering, line breaks, lists) and avoid cramming a large block of text together. Mind the spacing around formulas; fractions, radicals, and superscripts/subscripts should be expressed correctly.

### Answers and Solutions

- **Complete and correct answers**: objective questions (fill-in-the-blank, multiple choice, true/false, equivalent expressions, etc.) must provide accurate answers, otherwise the system cannot auto-grade them.
- **Note tolerance for fill-in-the-blanks**: fill-in-the-blank questions should state whether **tolerance requirements** apply (such as exact match, numeric tolerance ±0.01, percentage tolerance, constant evaluation) to avoid misjudgment from precision issues.
- **Make multiple choice modes explicit**: clearly state whether a choice question is **single choice, multiple choice, or indefinite choice**, and provide complete options. For multiple choice or indefinite choice, explain the scoring rules (such as how to handle omissions or wrong selections) to avoid disputes.
- **Solutions may be optional, but should not be empty**: the solution is an optional field, but a good solution makes a question more valuable. If you truly cannot provide a solution or answer, explicitly mark it as "missing" and leave it to the reviewers. For subjective questions such as proofs and word problems, it is recommended to attach a **standard or reference answer** to facilitate grading.

### Splitting Multi-Part Questions

- **Split sensibly**: when a large question is split into several sub-questions, the sub-questions should have a clear progressive or parallel relationship and each should be independently answerable and gradable. Avoid forcing unrelated questions into a single multi-part question.
- **Provide answers for each part**: every sub-question should have a corresponding answer and scoring basis, avoiding an incomplete large question where "some parts have no answers."
- **Mark point values (if applicable)**: if the question will be used in competitions or paper assembly, be sure to set reasonable sub-question point values and scoring rules in the question configuration.

::: tip What Makes a Good Question
A good question is not merely one that "can be solved"; it has **clear tested points, workable data, a unique answer, and can be explained when wrong**. Reading the statement from a solver's perspective after writing it will often reveal many problems.
:::

## 3. Format and Submission Standards

When contributing a question (path: the "Contribute Question" entry in the navigation), fill in the fields as follows:

- **Title**: concise and reflective of the question's content. Avoid meaningless titles (such as "Question 1" or "made up casually") or titles unrelated to the subject.
- **Subject and sub-discipline**: choose the correct subject. Mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, geography, and history are further divided into sub-disciplines; choose the closest fit (such as "Geometry", "Mechanics", "Ancient Chinese History").
- **Grade level**: choose according to the question's actual applicable grade range (lower elementary / upper elementary / middle school / high school). Note that physics, chemistry, geography, history, and politics do not have elementary school levels; follow what the platform displays when choosing.
- **Difficulty stars**: honestly choose the **basic difficulty** (gimme / easy / medium / hard / extremely hard); STEM subjects may additionally choose a **competition difficulty** (comp- up to IMO+). The determination standards for each level (including subject-specific anchors) are in the [Difficulty Star Rating Standards](/en/academic/difficulty). Deliberately inflating or deflating the difficulty interferes with the site-wide difficulty search and learning recommendations and counts as improper behavior.
- **Question type**: choose according to the question's actual answering mode (fill-in-the-blank / multiple choice / equivalent expression / true-false / proof / word problem / short answer / essay / listening, etc.). Different subjects support different question types; follow what the platform displays.
- **Tags**: choose fitting knowledge-point tags; if no suitable tag exists, you may propose a new one. Tags are an important basis for search and learning recommendations; **do not use tags unrelated to the question** to pad the count.
- **Editor mode**: both visual mode and code mode are acceptable; both save as Markdown and can be switched at any time. For STEM formulas, the **visual formula input** feature is recommended to ensure correct LaTeX from the start.

::: warning Content Safety
Question content must not contain any illegal, sensitive, pornographic, violent, or discriminatory material, and must not touch on politically sensitive topics. Such content will be rejected outright once discovered and may incur serious penalties (see the [Community Rules and Penalties](/en/policies/rule)).
:::

### Questions Already Covered by the Calculation Zone (Not Accepted)

**Contributions of problems that the Calculation Zone already fully supports are no longer accepted.** The Calculation Zone (see the [Calculation Zone Guide](/en/basic/calc)) covers nine types of calculation questions — mental math, clever four-operation arithmetic, integral expressions, linear equations in one variable, factorization, linear inequalities in one variable, systems of linear inequalities, quadratic equations in one variable, and systems of linear equations — all **auto-generated and auto-graded** by the system, with any number of questions reproducible by category / difficulty / options. These knowledge points already have complete functional support, so contributing similar problems to the question bank:

- **Wastes review resources**: the system can generate the same kind of problems instantly; neither manual question-setting by contributors nor question-by-question review by administrators is necessary;
- **Dilutes question bank quality**: simple calculation problems mixed into the bank interfere with difficulty search and learning recommendations.

Therefore, problems that fall within the nine Calculation Zone types and do not exceed its capabilities **will be directly rejected during review**. Please practice in the [Calculation Zone](/en/basic/calc) instead, or generate the corresponding calculation question group through [Paper Generation](/en/basic/paper). If you believe a problem goes beyond the Calculation Zone's support scope (for example, it involves a more complex solution or special requirements), state this in the title or tags when submitting so administrators can judge.

::: danger Repeated Submission
Repeatedly resubmitting rejected calculation problems counts as wasting administrative resources and will be handled under the [Community Rules and Penalties](/en/policies/rule), possibly resulting in revoked contribution permission.
:::

## 4. Attachment Standards

Questions support uploading image attachments and listening audio attachments; attachments should serve the question itself:

- **Image attachments**: when uploading **statement illustrations / figures / solution images**, make sure the images are clear, correctly oriented, and match the question's content. Images intended for the solution will be displayed as part of this question's solution. Do not upload images unrelated to the question, memes, or ads.
- **Listening audio** (English listening questions only): upload **audio-only** listening audio (max 50 MB) and fill in the **listening material** (the listening transcript); the two should be consistent in content. The listening audio should be clear, free of noise, and free of spoilers (do not reveal answers before the questions are read).
- **File source**: attachments should be self-made or authorized material. When screenshotting others' materials, confirm that there are no copyright or privacy issues.
- **Attachments consistent with the statement**: illustrations should match the description in the statement, and listening audio should match the listening material. An attachment that contradicts the statement will directly make the question unanswerable.

## 5. Review and Revision Process

After submission, a question goes through the following process. Please be aware of it and cooperate:

1. **Pending review**: after submission, the system automatically generates a question number (such as `M0001`) and the question enters the review queue.
2. **Administrator review**: an administrator examines the statement, answer, solution, and attachments in the review console and makes an **approve (publish)** or **reject (with reason)** decision.
3. **Check the result**: view the approved / rejected status and the rejection reason on the "My Contributions" page.
4. **Revise and resubmit**: when rejected or in need of changes, update the question content and resubmit; the question will **return to the pending review state** and require another review.
5. **Fully traceable**: review actions (reviewer, time, reason) are recorded throughout.

### Revision Suggestions (New Versions)

For published questions, in addition to direct editing, you may submit a **revision suggestion** (a complete new version):

- Revision suggestions go through the **same review process** and replace the live question once approved.
- Historical revision suggestions can be viewed and traced, so a revision is not "sneakily changing it" but a transparent version replacement.
- When a question is reported or an administrator finds a content issue, correcting it via a revision suggestion is preferred over setting a brand new question; it is the more reasonable approach.

### Appeals

For a rejected question contribution, if you believe the review was incorrect, you can initiate an **appeal** to request re-review. The appeal enters the ticket system, where an administrator re-processes it and gives a final result.

::: tip Appeal Process
For the specific operations of appeals and tickets, see the "Content Appeals" and "Reporting & Ticket System" sections of the [Account & Security Guide](/en/basic/account-safety).
:::

## 6. Problem Set Standards

A problem set is a collection of questions organized by theme, suitable as a question source for focused practice, exam-prep sharing, competitions, and homework. When creating a problem set, note the following:

- **Clear theme**: the title and description should clearly state the problem set's theme and target audience (such as "Grade 8 Math: Linear Functions Special Topic"). Avoid titles that do not match the content or whose meaning is unclear.
- **Relevant content**: the questions in a problem set should be relevant to its theme. Stuffing unrelated questions into a problem set (especially to pad the count) counts as a low-quality contribution, and a public problem set may be rejected for it.
- **Reasonable order**: use drag-and-drop ordering so the questions progress step by step and form a learning path; you may add notes to questions.
- **Tags and subject**: fill in the subject and tags correctly to facilitate search.
- **Do not copy others' problem sets**: compiling content from another person's already-public problem set requires noting the referenced source; do not wholesale copy someone else's problem set and publish it under your own name.
- **Public sets require review**: after submission, a public problem set enters pending review and becomes visible site-wide once approved by an administrator. If you only want to share within a small circle, you can set it to private and link it to a designated team.
- **Timely maintenance**: if a question referenced by a problem set has been modified or delisted, check whether the problem set can still be practiced normally.
- **Reusability**: problem sets often serve as the question source for competitions, team homework, and paper assembly (questions can be inherited from a problem set with one click), so make sure the question quality is solid, because your problem set will be used by others to assemble papers and assign homework.

::: warning Malicious Behavior
**Malicious contributions** (such as batch-submitting junk questions, deliberately submitting duplicate or meaningless content, padding problem sets with unrelated questions, or plagiarizing others' problem sets) not only waste administrators' review resources but also count as "wasting the site's administrative resources," and will be handled under the [Community Rules and Penalties](/en/policies/rule), possibly resulting in **revoked relevant permissions** or even a ban.
:::

## 7. Consequences of Violations

Violations involving questions and problem sets are handled in tiers by severity:

| Case | Handling |
| --- | --- |
| Unclear statement, formatting errors, improper tags | Review rejection with reason, returned for revision |
| Submitting calculation problems already covered by the Calculation Zone | Review rejection with reason (see "Questions Already Covered by the Calculation Zone" above) |
| Repeatedly submitting low-quality / meaningless questions | Warning; severe cases revoke contribution permission |
| Plagiarizing questions, uploading infringing content | Warning → mute → ban (per severity and repeat offenses) |
| Malicious spam, batch junk contributions | Mute → account ban |
| Repeated violations, habitual offenders | Heavier penalties under the [violation period](/en/policies/rule) |

All penalties are recorded in the form of **tickets** and can be appealed. A contributor earns points only on the premise that the content is genuine and valid: points are awarded only when a question / problem set contribution **passes review** (see the [Points & Benefits Guide](/en/basic/rewards)); plagiarized or invalid contributions earn no points and may instead cost you the eligibility to contribute.

::: danger Do Not Count on Luck
The violation period records every violation and automatically computes repeat offenses. Do not try to evade penalties with multiple accounts; the **joint liability rule** will additionally penalize all linked accounts (see the [Community Rules and Penalties](/en/policies/rule)).
:::

## FAQ

**Q: I adapted a question from a textbook; does it count as original?**
A: If the core structure, tested points, and data are substantially the same, it is an adaptation rather than an original, and the source should be cited; if you only referenced its tested points and designed a fresh question, it is original. When in doubt, it is recommended to cite the source.

**Q: Will repeatedly resubmitting a rejected question make it pass?**
A: No. Resubmitting the same question without changing the content is treated as wasting review resources. Please revise according to the rejection reason first, then resubmit.

**Q: Does the listening audio have to be self-recorded?**
A: No, but it must be audio you are authorized to use and whose content matches the listening material. Audio from paid materials or recorded elsewhere requires confirmed authorization.

**Q: Can others edit my problem set?**
A: By default, a problem set is maintained by its creator. If you want multi-person collaboration, you can link it to a team and share management within the team.

**Q: What should I do if my question is reported?**
A: A report generates a ticket; an administrator re-checks it and handles it. If you believe the report is untrue or the handling improper, you can appeal through the ticket system.
