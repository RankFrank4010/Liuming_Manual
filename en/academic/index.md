# Academic Standards Overview

LiuMing is a practice and learning community covering all grades from elementary to high school across nine subjects (mathematics, Chinese, physics, chemistry, biology, English, geography, history, and politics). It brings together learning resources accumulated by the community, such as question banks, problem sets, and full paper libraries, as well as collaborative spaces like competitions, teams, columns, and discussion forums.

The richer the resources and activities, the more we need a shared set of standards to guarantee their quality and credibility. **Academic standards** exist precisely for this purpose: they define the behavioral boundaries for all kinds of academic activities, including questions, solutions, articles, AI assistance, and competitions, protecting every user who takes setting questions, answering questions, or writing articles seriously, and making sure every piece of learning material on LiuMing stands up to scrutiny.

## Why Academic Standards Are Needed

Academic standards exist first of all to **safeguard academic integrity**. If a question is copied outright, or a solution is merely patched together from wrong content, both directly mislead the students who practice with them later and pollute the whole question bank. If a competition ranking is polluted by proxy taking or cheating, it is unfair to every participant who competed seriously.

Second, they exist to **ensure fairness**. Competitions have uniform rules, grading follows a uniform process, and reporting and appeals go through a unified channel. Standards make every step predictable, traceable, and appealable, rather than something handled arbitrarily by personal preference.

Finally, they exist to **ensure quality**. Standards are not shackles but directional guidance built on top of a baseline. They tell contributors how to craft a good question, write a good solution, and organize a good competition, so the community's accumulated knowledge grows ever more solid.

::: tip The Relationship Between Standards and Community Rules
Academic standards are the concretization of the [Community Rules and Penalties](/en/policies/rule) within academic activities. Penalty types and processes (warnings, mutes, forum bans, account bans, violation periods, joint liability, etc.) follow the community rules; this section explains how those rules apply to academic scenarios.
:::

## Core Principles

LiuMing's academic standards revolve around four principles, and every clause in this section is an embodiment of them.

### Standardization

Standards make content searchable, reviewable, and reusable. Questions follow a fixed system of subject, grade level, question type, and difficulty; problem sets have themes and tags; articles follow conventions for structure and formatting. Without these conventions, a flood of content would only become a tangled mess. For contributors, standardization means "there are rules to follow": write according to the standard and the chances of acceptance go up. For learners, standardization means "predictability": no matter which material you pick up, you know how to use it.

### Originality

The material accumulated by the community should come from genuine thought and creation. Originality does not require that every sentence be unprecedented; what it requires is that citations be marked, borrowings be acknowledged, and plagiarism be prohibited. The value of a question bank rests precisely on the premise that "every question can be traced to its source and trusted." Once originality is lost, the question bank degenerates into a pile of reposted text that no one is responsible for.

### Traceability

Every governance action on LiuMing is traceable: a question's review log records the reviewer and the reason, the ticket system generates an event timeline for every report, appeal, and ban, and violation periods centrally record each violation. Traceability constrains both the party being disciplined (they cannot deny it) and the party doing the disciplining (they cannot operate behind closed doors). When a problem arises, going through the records restores the facts.

### Fair Competition

Competition results must truthfully reflect ability. One account per person, no proxy taking, no sharing answers; everyone is equal before the rules. The value of a competition comes from fairness: only when every participant's ranking depends solely on their own ability does standing at the top of the leaderboard deserve pride, and only then will spectators believe in the meaning of that ranking.

## Scope of Application

The standards in this section apply to all behavior on LiuMing related to academic content, including but not limited to:

- Contributing questions to the question bank and submitting revision suggestions (new versions).
- Creating and making public problem sets.
- Posting in the discussion forum and publishing articles and solutions in columns.
- Using generative AI to assist learning, writing, or grading.
- Creating competitions, registering to participate, or serving as a grader or question setter.
- Uploading full papers to the full paper library.

Even if a behavior is not explicitly listed, as long as it damages academic integrity, content quality, or community fairness, administrators may handle it in accordance with the principles of the [Community Rules and Penalties](/en/policies/rule).

::: danger Violation Period and Repeat Offenses
Penalties are not one-time events. According to the [Community Rules and Penalties](/en/policies/rule), the 365 days after a first offense constitute a violation period; a repeat violation within that period extends the period and increases the penalty. Repeatedly violating the same rule will result in a finding of **repeat offender (habitual offender)**, with penalties escalating each time. Do not adopt an "a minor violation once in a while is no big deal" attitude; every violation you commit will be recorded.
:::

## Violation and Penalty Overview

Depending on the severity of the violation, violations in academic activities may incur the following penalties (see the [Community Rules and Penalties](/en/policies/rule) for details):

| Violation Type | Common Cases | Possible Penalties |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minor violation | Unclear question statement, messy formatting, incorrect tags | Review rejection with reasons, returned for revision |
| General violation | Reposting others' solutions without attribution, low-quality spam content | Warning, mute |
| Serious violation | Plagiarizing questions or problem sets, malicious submissions, cheating, attacking the system | Forum ban, account ban |
| Especially serious | Copyright infringement, disrupting platform operations, refusing to change despite repeated admonition | Permanent ban, with joint liability on linked accounts where necessary |

Penalties are not the goal; correction is. The vast majority of minor violations are already stopped at the review stage: rejected questions come with reasons, and fixing and resubmitting is all that is needed. What really escalates to a ban is usually repeated, deliberate, and malicious behavior. The platform reserves an appeal channel for every user: rejected contributions can be appealed, banned accounts can appeal, and unfair treatment can be appealed.

::: tip What to Do After Being Rejected or Penalized
A rejected question or problem set contribution can initiate an **appeal** to request re-review; a banned user can submit an appeal and supplementary materials through a **ban ticket**. See the [Account & Security Guide](/en/basic/account-safety) for the specific appeal process.
:::

## FAQ

**Q: If I accidentally violate a rule, will I be banned directly?**
A: No. Penalties are graded by severity, and a first minor violation usually results in a warning or a review rejection. Only serious violations, repeated violations (habitual offenders), or deliberate destructive behavior escalate to a ban. Moreover, all penalties can be appealed.

**Q: Does borrowing from someone else's solution count as plagiarism?**
A: Rewriting another person's approach in your own words after learning from it, while **citing the source**, is normal exchange; copying whole passages without attribution is plagiarism. The difference lies in whether you honestly disclose the source.

**Q: If I use AI to polish my article, do I need to tell people?**
A: Yes. As long as you used AI assistance in your creative work, you should disclose its specific contribution at the end of the article. This is not embarrassing; it is a responsible thing to do. See the [Generative AI Usage Standards](/en/academic/ai).

**Q: Does a rejected question still have a chance to pass?**
A: Yes. Modify it according to the rejection reason and resubmit; if you believe the review was wrong, you can initiate an appeal. The review process is fully traceable, and every round comes with a clear reason.

**Q: Do the standards change?**
A: Yes. Like the [Community Rules and Penalties](/en/policies/rule), this section is updated as circumstances change. Important adjustments involving academic activities are synced to the corresponding pages; it is advisable to keep an eye on them before contributing to the community.

## Notes for Contributors

Before participating in academic contributions (setting questions, building problem sets, writing articles, hosting competitions), please confirm the following:

- **You have read the corresponding standard**: read the [Problem & Problem Set Standards](/en/academic/problem) before setting questions, and the [Solution & Article Standards](/en/academic/article) before publishing articles. The standards are short, but they will help you avoid detours.
- **You understand the review process**: questions, public problem sets, and public competitions all require administrator review, which takes time; please be patient. A rejection does not mean it is targeted at you; simply revise according to the reason.
- **You understand the points rules**: points are only awarded when a question or problem set contribution **passes review**, and points can be exchanged for benefits such as credits (see the [Points & Benefits Guide](/en/basic/rewards)). Quality is the prerequisite for earning points, not quantity.
- **You are willing to take responsibility for your content**: content published to the community will exist for a long time and be read by many later users. Every line you write must do justice to your readers.
