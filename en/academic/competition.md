# Competition Standards

Competitions are LiuMing's online contest feature: an organizer runs a contest, participants answer online, and ability is tested against the leaderboard. The meaning of a competition lies in **fair competition**; only a genuine ranking deserves recognition. This page defines the standards that both organizers and participants must follow.

## 1. Organizer Responsibilities

Competitions are initiated by their organizers, who are responsible for the quality and fairness of the entire competition.

### Original Question Setting

- **Original question setting**: competition questions must be consistent with the [Problem & Problem Set Standards](/en/academic/problem), satisfy originality requirements, and must not be plagiarized or include unauthorized materials.
- **Complete statements and answers**: every question should have a complete and correct statement, answer, and solution. Objective questions must be auto-gradable by the system; subjective questions must allow graders (or self-grading participants) to score accurately against the answer.
- **Self-consistent data**: data must be verified to avoid invalidating an entire competition because of a question error.
- **AI question setting boundary**: when using AI to assist question setting, questions must pass strict human verification (see the [Generative AI Usage Standards](/en/academic/ai)).
- **Collaborative question setting**: organizers may invite **question setters** to jointly maintain questions; question setters bear equal responsibility for question quality, and invitees' question-setting ability and integrity records should be vetted.

### Grading Methods

When creating a competition, you must choose a **grading method**, and the three methods carry different responsibility terms:

- **Organizer-only grading**: all subjective questions are scored only by the organizer. The organizer should complete all grading within a reasonable time after the competition ends and update the leaderboard promptly.
- **Designated graders**: the organizer designates multiple graders to score together. Graders should score fairly according to the standard answer and **must not show favoritism toward themselves or acquaintances**; the organizer is responsible for coordination and re-checking.
- **Participant self-grading**: participants grade their own subjective answers. The organizer should provide a clear standard answer in the question configuration and emphasize the integrity requirements of self-grading in the competition description.

::: danger Unfair Grading Is a Serious Violation
A grader deliberately giving wrong scores to specific participants (favoritism or suppression) damages competition fairness and will incur **warnings, mutes**, or even **bans**. If participants notice clearly unfair grading, they can report it through the report and appeal channels.
:::

### Public Competition Review

- **Public competitions require review**: public (or team-visible and other review-required) competitions must be reviewed by an administrator before publication, covering question quality, description, and rule statements.
- **Content compliance**: the competition title, description, and questions must not contain illegal, sensitive, discriminatory, or advertising content, or the competition will be refused publication.
- **State the rules**: organizers should specify the competition's time, answering mode, scoring rules, and notes in the description to avoid disputes caused by unclear information.

### Competition Management

- Organizers may invite **question setters** to jointly maintain questions; question setters are equally responsible for question quality.
- After the competition ends, the organizer should complete the wrap-up: finish grading all subjective questions, export the paper (if needed), etc.
- Competition questions can be inherited for paper assembly or team homework, so question quality affects not only this competition but also later reuse.

## 2. Participant Code of Conduct

Participants should follow the code below to maintain the authenticity and fairness of competitions:

### One Person, One Account

- **One person, one account**: each participant may compete only with their own account, and each participant can have only one identity.
- **No proxy taking**: do not let others answer in your place, and do not answer in others' place. Both parties involved in proxy taking will be dealt with.
- **No account sharing**: do not share accounts or borrow others' accounts to compete. Sneaking a borrowed account into the designated participant list of a private competition is also a violation.

### Independent Thinking

- **No plagiarism**: do not copy others' submissions and do not share answers with others.
- **No AI answering in your place**: competition submissions should reflect your own ability. When a competition question involves calculation, proof, writing, or code-based answers, you must not directly submit AI-generated answers. Objective questions are graded by the system and subjective questions are scored by graders; AI-generated submissions, lacking traces of personal thinking, will appear distorted, and once confirmed, will be treated as cheating.

::: warning Note
LiuMing's platform itself provides assistive features such as AI grading and AI solutions, but the **answering stage of a competition** requires participants to complete it independently. Using learning materials for pre-competition prep or post-competition review is fine, but answers submitted during the competition must be your genuine work.
:::

### In-Competition Behavior

- **No exchanging answers**: while a competition is running, do not exchange or ask for answers through any channel, including the discussion forum, teams, or private messages. Leaking questions and checking answers are both violations; both the initiators and the spreaders will be dealt with.
- **Respect the time**: answer within the competition's set start / end times; late submissions or post-competition tampering with submissions will not be recognized.
- **No attacking the system**: do not attack, reverse-engineer, or scrape competition data; do not attempt to obtain question answers, grading results, or others' submissions; do not interfere with the competition system's operation in any way. Such behavior is handled as "deliberately wasting site resources," with consequences detailed below.

## 3. Consequences of Cheating

Once confirmed, cheating will be handled under the [Community Rules and Penalties](/en/policies/rule):

| Case | Handling |
| --- | --- |
| Plagiarism / sharing answers with others | Relevant results voided, warning → mute |
| Proxy taking, account sharing, competing under another's name | Results voided, mute → account ban |
| Exchanging / spreading answers during the competition | Results of those involved voided, warning → mute (initiators penalized more heavily) |
| Using AI to answer in your place | Results voided, warning → mute |
| Attacking the system, scraping data, reverse engineering | **Permanent ban**, with joint liability on linked accounts where necessary |
| Repeated cheating, habitual offenders | Heavier penalties under the [violation period](/en/policies/rule) |

::: danger Joint Liability
If you cheat using multiple accounts, or register and use other accounts to evade penalties after being penalized, all linked accounts will incur equivalent or heavier additional penalties; for frequent violations from the same source, the platform will also impose access restrictions. Do not count on luck.
:::

All penalties are recorded in the form of **ban tickets** and can be appealed. If a cheating report is confirmed, the reported person's results are voided; if an investigation finds the report to be a malicious false accusation, the reporter will likewise be dealt with.

## 4. Re-run Competitions and Series

- **Re-run competitions**: organizers can open a re-run of a finished competition, cloning the original competition's questions and configuration to hold a new round (see the [Competition Guide](/en/basic/competition)). Re-run questions are equally subject to this standard; making one public still requires administrator review.
- **Series rules**: when running a series (such as a semi-final and final), the relationship between rounds should be clear. Questions in different rounds should have their quality verified independently; do not skip review just because "the questions carry over from the previous round."
- **Result independence**: each competition's ranking is computed independently, and a re-run does not represent a continuation of historical results. Do not exploit the re-run mechanism to fabricate "past record."
- **Time settings**: re-runs should set reasonable start / end times to avoid conflicts with or ambiguity about the official competition's schedule.

## 5. Reporting and Appeals

- **Report cheating**: if you find someone proxy-taking, plagiarizing, exchanging answers, or attacking the system, submit through the report feature (or the ticket system) with evidence; administrators will review and handle it.
- **Report question problems**: if you find errors, plagiarism, or violating content in competition questions, you can report those as well.
- **Appeals**: if you disagree with a grading result, or believe you were wrongly judged / penalized, you can appeal through the ticket system. Banned users submit appeals and supplementary materials through ban tickets (see the [Account & Security Guide](/en/basic/account-safety)).

::: tip Tips for Participants
Before registering for a public competition, read the rules and schedule in the competition description; check the grading results promptly after the competition ends, and appeal early if you have objections. Respect every competition, and respect every opponent.
:::

## 6. Organizing a Competition: A Checklist

Before the competition, organizers can self-check against the following list to avoid common pitfalls:

- [ ] Have all questions been verified: data self-consistent, answers correct, unambiguous, and no plagiarism?
- [ ] Are the graders for subjective questions confirmed, and has a grading completion time been agreed?
- [ ] Has the public competition been submitted for review, with enough review time reserved?
- [ ] Does the competition description clearly state the time, rules, scoring method, and notes?
- [ ] After the competition, are grading wrap-up and result confirmation arranged?

## 7. Rewards and Points

LiuMing competitions have no built-in ranking reward mechanism, but as community activities, competitions also benefit from the community contribution rules:

- If questions in a competition are also contributed to the question bank and pass review, points will be awarded per question bank contribution (see the [Points & Benefits Guide](/en/basic/rewards)).
- High-quality public competitions are a source of community reputation. Hosting a competition carries no points reward, so it should be organized from a genuine intention to "serve the community," rather than chasing rankings or vanity.
- Do not conduct any form of trading, charging, or disguised profit-making in the name of a competition; do not use a competition as an excuse to run activities unrelated to learning.

::: tip The Heart of Hosting a Competition
The value of a good competition is letting participants test their learning through fair competition. Setting good questions, explaining the rules clearly, and grading fairly is the best contribution to the community.
:::

## FAQ

**Q: I am an organizer; can I choose competition questions directly from the question bank?**
A: Yes. Competition questions can be chosen from the question bank or customized on the spot. Choosing from the question bank reuses reviewed questions, so quality is better assured; custom questions require you to keep the quality in check yourself.

**Q: Does withdrawing mid-competition affect my results?**
A: You can withdraw at any time. Withdrawn participants are no longer counted in the ranking; how already-submitted answers are handled depends on the competition description.

**Q: Will friends grading each other's subjective answers be considered cheating?**
A: When "participant self-grading" or "designated graders" is chosen, grading itself is a normal mechanism; but if a grader deliberately gives high scores to close connections and low scores to others, that is favoritism and will be dealt with.

**Q: I suspect someone is cheating; what is the most effective way to report?**
A: Provide specific evidence as much as possible (abnormal answering times, answering style inconsistent with the person, screenshots of answer exchanges, etc.) and submit it through the report feature. Reports with evidence are more likely to be verified.

**Q: If I accidentally see someone posting answers in the discussion forum during the competition, am I responsible?**
A: If you only saw it and did not spread it, you will usually not be penalized; but if you used those answers, your results will be voided. The right approach is to ignore it and report the answer leaker.

**Q: Do team competitions follow the same rules as public competitions?**
A: The core rules are the same (question setting, answering, cheating handling); team competitions add team-level management terms: team administrators should be responsible for member management, private question review, and competition order, and if a team commits serious violations, the team itself may be banned (see the joint liability clause of the [Community Rules and Penalties](/en/policies/rule)).
