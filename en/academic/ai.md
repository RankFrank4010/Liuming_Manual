# Generative AI Usage Standards

Generative AI is rapidly changing the way people learn and create. On LiuMing, AI can be both a capable learning assistant (AI grading, AI solutions, AI learning analysis) and a shortcut that undermines academic integrity (directly using AI to submit homework, set questions, or write articles). This page defines the behavioral boundaries for using generative AI to assist learning and creation.

The core principle in one sentence: **AI is a tool, not the author.** You may use AI for assistance, but the final approach, reasoning, and responsibility must belong to you.

## 1. Where AI Assistance Is Allowed

The following scenarios are AI-assisted uses that LiuMing encourages or permits:

- **Polishing and typesetting**: use AI to refine wording, fix typos, and standardize formatting.
- **Generating drafts**: use AI to produce a first draft, then use it after your own understanding, revision, and verification.
- **Checking and verifying**: use AI to check calculation steps, verify proof processes, and find logic holes.
- **Learning assistance**: use AI to explain concepts, organize knowledge structures, and generate practice suggestions.
- **Platform-provided AI features**: using the platform's **AI grading** (scoring subjective answers and returning feedback), **AI solutions** (viewing explanations after unlocking), and **AI learning analysis** is part of the platform's service and can be used normally.

::: tip About Platform AI Features
AI grading and AI solutions are uniformly provided by the platform and are only for assisting learning and grading; for the final judgment, rely on your own understanding. For the relevant mechanics, see the [Grading Center Guide](/en/basic/grading) and the [Question Bank Guide](/en/basic/bank).
:::

## 2. Do Not Directly Copy AI Output

The following content must not be copied directly from AI output:

- **Key approaches and reasoning**: the core approach of a solution, the reasoning chain of a proof, and the central thesis of an article must come from your own thinking.
- **Code**: if a homework or competition answer involves code, the key algorithms and implementation should be done by yourself; AI-generated code may only be used after full understanding and verification, and must be disclosed.
- **Complete articles**: do not publish AI-generated articles or solutions as-is, passing them off as your own creation.
- **Questions**: do not submit AI-generated questions to the question bank without review (see "AI Grading ≠ AI Question Writing" below).

## 3. Disclosure Obligation

**Whenever AI assistance is used in content creation, you must honestly disclose the specific contribution of the AI at the end of the work.**

- Examples: "This article was polished with AI assistance," "The solving approach and proof are my own; AI was used to check the calculations," "The draft was generated by AI and proofread and corrected by me."
- Disclosure is not admitting to "cheating"; it is **academic transparency**. Letting others believe the content is entirely yours while not disclosing is itself a dishonest act.
- There is no need for additional disclosure when using the platform's built-in AI features (AI grading, AI solutions, AI learning analysis), because their results are clearly marked as generated by the platform's AI.

::: danger Consequences of Non-Disclosure
Using AI assistance in questions, solutions, articles, or competition answers without disclosing it is treated as concealing the source of creation. If this is accompanied by copying AI output or passing AI content off as original, it will be handled as plagiarism or malicious submission (see the [Problem & Problem Set Standards](/en/academic/problem) and the [Solution & Article Standards](/en/academic/article)).
:::

## 4. Author Responsibility

No matter how much AI helped, **the author bears full responsibility for the correctness, originality, and compliance of the content**:

- AI makes mistakes, and it can also fabricate things with complete confidence (hallucinations). Adopting unverified AI output directly is the responsibility of the publisher, not of the AI.
- "The AI wrote it" cannot excuse wrong, plagiarized, or violating content.
- If copying AI output leads to incorrect or infringing content, the penalty is exactly the same as when AI is not used, or even heavier (because it could have been verified).

## 5. Malicious Submissions Count as Violations

The following behaviors constitute **malicious submissions** and will be handled under the [Community Rules and Penalties](/en/policies/rule):

- Batch-generating questions with AI and submitting them to the question bank without screening, creating large amounts of junk or erroneous content.
- Publishing AI-generated wrong solutions as correct content and misleading readers.
- Using AI to farm practice, farm grading, or generate large numbers of meaningless replies, wasting platform compute and administrator resources.
- Using AI to answer in place of yourself in competitions (see the [Competition Standards](/en/academic/competition)).

::: tip Distinguishing "Ability" from "Laziness"
It is easy for AI to produce a seemingly complete solution, but understanding it, verifying it, and being able to explain it to others is where learning actually happens. Directly pasting AI output deceives yourself, not just the reviewers.
:::

## 6. AI Grading ≠ AI Question Writing

This is a boundary that needs special emphasis.

- **AI grading is a platform service**: for subjective answers in specific subjects, the platform can invoke AI to auto-grade and return scores and feedback (see the [Grading Center Guide](/en/basic/grading)). This assists the **grading** stage and serves questions originally created by humans.
- **Questions must be designed by humans**: questions in the question bank must be original human work (see the [Problem & Problem Set Standards](/en/academic/problem)). AI may assist with brainstorming and polishing the statement, but **the core design of a question (tested points, context, data, answer) should be completed by a human**.
- **AI-generated questions require strict human verification**: if you do use AI to assist question setting, you must **verify item by item** before submission: whether the answer is correct, the data self-consistent, the wording unambiguous, the difficulty matched, and whether multiple solutions exist. AI questions that have not been strictly verified must not be submitted.
- **The same applies to competition question setting**: competition questions likewise require human oversight, and unverified AI questions must not be passed off as original question setting.

::: warning The Boundary in One Sentence
"AI grading" grades **human-set questions**; "AI question writing" produces questions that must **pass strict human verification**. Treating AI questions as original question setting, or treating AI output as the standard answer, crosses this line.
:::

## 7. Handling Violations

| Case | Handling |
| --- | --- |
| AI assistance used but honestly disclosed, content correct | Compliant, no action needed |
| AI assistance used but not disclosed (no other problems) | Ordered to supplement disclosure, warning |
| AI output copied and passed off as original | Content deleted, warning → mute |
| AI-batch-submitted junk / erroneous content | Mute → account ban, counted into the [violation period](/en/policies/rule) |
| AI used to answer in place of yourself in a competition | Results voided, warning → mute → ban (see the [Competition Standards](/en/academic/competition)) |

## 8. AI Usage Self-Checklist

Before submitting any content, you can quickly self-check against the following list:

- [ ] Do I fully understand the approach and process of this content, rather than just copying it?
- [ ] Have I verified its calculations, proofs, and conclusions?
- [ ] Have I disclosed the specific contribution of the AI at the end of the work?
- [ ] If the content is a question, have I confirmed that the tested points, data, answer, and difficulty all passed human verification?
- [ ] If it is for a competition, have I confirmed that the submission was completed entirely by myself?

If any item cannot be checked off, the content is not ready to be submitted. Add the verification and disclosure first, then publish.

## 9. Example Boundary Scenarios

The following scenarios help illustrate the boundaries of the rules, for reference:

| Scenario | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Using AI to polish a solution you wrote yourself | Allowed; just disclose it |
| AI generates the full solution, published as-is | Violation (copying and passing off as original) |
| AI generates the full solution; you read it through, understand it, fix errors, publish, and disclose at the end | Allowed, but must disclose and take responsibility for the content |
| AI generates a question, submitted to the question bank without verification | Violation (malicious / low-quality submission) |
| AI generates a question; you verify it item by item and submit, noting AI's involvement | Allowed |
| Using AI to generate all answers and submit them in a competition | Violation (results voided) |
| Using AI after the competition to review your own submission | Allowed |
| Using the AI grading feature to grade your own subjective answers | Allowed (platform feature, marked as AI-generated) |

The core of the judgment is always three things: **whether you copied, whether you disclosed, and whether you took responsibility**.

## FAQ

**Q: Does using AI to check my calculations count as a violation?**
A: No. Using AI for checking and verification is an allowed assistive use; just explain it per the disclosure obligation.

**Q: AI wrote a first draft of my article and I heavily revised it; do I still need to disclose?**
A: Yes. As long as AI participated in the creation process, you should disclose it. Disclosure is an honest statement of "how much you did" and "how much AI helped."

**Q: How do I judge whether an AI-generated question can be submitted?**
A: Treat the AI question as a "draft for review" rather than a "finished product." After verifying the answer, data, ambiguity, difficulty, and multiple-solution issues item by item, submit only when everything checks out. Do not submit questions you cannot verify.

**Q: If AI grading's conclusion differs from the teacher's, whose should I follow?**
A: AI grading is an aid; the final judgment should be based on your own understanding, the solution, and authoritative sources. The platform also offers an invited grading option (asking trusted people to grade).

**Q: Can I use AI to organize my mistake notebook?**
A: Yes. Organizing mistakes and generating analysis reports count as learning assistance and are allowed; the key is that you understand the analysis AI outputs, rather than directly treating the AI report as your own learning conclusion.
