# Solution & Article Standards

LiuMing's discussion forum is used to exchange learning insights and question solutions; columns are used to publish articles and share experience and knowledge. Whether it is a post in the discussion forum or a long article in a column, in essence you are passing knowledge on to others.

The prerequisite for passing on knowledge is **correctness**. A wrong solution teaches readers something wrong, and a perfunctory share wastes readers' time. This page defines the standards to follow when publishing content in the discussion forum and columns.

## 1. Content Quality

### Accuracy First

- **The solution must be correct**: before publishing a solution, confirm that the method itself is correct, the process has no skipped steps, and the conclusion is consistent with the question's standard answer. If you are not sure, state "for reference only, corrections welcome."
- **Honestly mark uncertainty**: for questions that are disputed or have multiple solution methods, you may introduce different approaches, but you should honestly state the premises and limitations of each, and not present guesses as settled conclusions.
- **Errata obligation**: if you discover an error in your own solution, correct or delete it as soon as possible to prevent the error from spreading. When correcting, you may note "corrected" in the body so later readers are aware of the version change.

::: danger Publishing a Wrong Solution Is Not "Freedom of Speech"
Deliberately publishing wrong solutions or using wrong content to mislead others disrupts academic order and will, depending on the circumstances, incur **warnings or mutes**. Making an unintentional error but refusing to correct it while continuing to mislead will likewise be handled.
:::

### Explain the Approach Clearly

A qualified solution should include at least the following:

- **Explanation of the approach**: why this method came to mind and where to start from the first step, rather than just dumping a pile of formulas. What readers need is not "what the answer is" but "how this answer was arrived at."
- **Necessary proofs**: provide a complete reasoning chain for proof questions; explain how each condition is used for word problems; provide the necessary argumentation for conclusions without skipping key steps.
- **Answer and verification**: give the final answer and, where possible, explain how to verify it (such as substitution checks or special-case testing) to help readers confirm the result.
- **Common mistake warnings (bonus)**: pointing out easy-to-fall-into pitfalls, common wrong approaches, and misconceptions is often the most helpful content for readers.

### Avoid Meaningless Content

- Do not post "give me the answer" / "what is the answer" help-and-spam posts that contain no substance (when asking for help, explain your own approach and where you are stuck).
- Do not post meme sprees, or replies such as plain "up" or "passing by" that carry no information.
- Do not post ads, promotional links, or content unrelated to subject learning.
- Do not repeatedly post identical or highly similar posts in the discussion forum.

## 2. Formatting Standards

Both the discussion forum and columns support Markdown and LaTeX formula typesetting; well-formatted content is easier to read and more welcome:

- **Clear structure**: long articles use headings to divide sections (## third-level headings), with the body organized into subsections. An article's structure should correspond to the question's steps so readers can follow it in order.
- **Use LaTeX for formulas**: formulas in STEM content should be typeset with the formula syntax (`$...$` for inline formulas, `$$...$$` for display formulas); do not write formulas as plain text or paste images.
- **Readable code**: if code or pseudocode is involved (such as algorithm explanations or programming approaches), use **code blocks** (``` fences) with the language marked, and keep indentation and naming conventions proper.
- **Collapse long code**: longer code should be stowed in **collapsible blocks** (details) so a screen full of code does not drown out the body text. The article should explain the key logic rather than making readers parse the entire block themselves.
- **Reasonable images**: illustrations should be clear and relevant to the content; for images such as handwritten solving processes, adjust orientation and brightness before uploading.
- **Precise titles**: the title of a post or article should summarize the content (such as "An Alternative Solution to M0001"), avoiding information-free titles like "help me take a look" or "need help."

::: tip The Standard of Good Typesetting
Good typesetting lets readers **grasp the whole article's thread just from the subheadings and formulas**. After writing, read it through yourself: are there paragraphs whose point cannot be seen at a glance? Are there formulas that should be typeset but are written as text?
:::

## 3. Originality and Citation

- **Do not plagiarize**: do not wholesale copy or rewrite others' (other users', websites', or publications') solutions or articles and publish them under your own name.
- **Cite sources when referencing**: when quoting others' methods, conclusions, proofs, or text, **clearly mark the source** (original author, link, book title, webpage, etc.). Attaching a link to the original when citing someone's solution is the most basic respect.
- **Do not infringe copyright**: do not upload whole copyrighted textbook content or the original text of paid materials. Sharing knowledge points is fine; wholesale copying passages is not.
- **Borrowing ideas is fine; copying text is not**: rewriting another person's approach in your own words after learning from it, with the referenced source noted, is healthy exchange; copy-pasting verbatim is not.

::: warning The Difference Between the Forum and Columns
Posts in the discussion forum are for exchange and discussion, so the tone can be relaxed; column articles are "publications" and should be more complete and more standard. Moving a low-quality post into a column as-is is inappropriate; column articles should meet the content and formatting standards listed on this page.
:::

## 4. AI Assistance Disclosure

Using generative AI to assist writing (polishing, typesetting, generating drafts, checking for errors) is allowed, but you must comply with the disclosure obligation:

- **Disclosure is mandatory**: if an article or solution used AI assistance, **honestly state the specific contribution of the AI** at the end (such as "This article was polished with AI" or "The solving approach is my own; AI assisted in checking the calculations").
- **Do not copy key content**: the core approach, reasoning, proofs, and code should come from your own thinking and verification, and must not be copied directly from AI output (see the [Generative AI Usage Standards](/en/academic/ai)).
- **The author is responsible**: whether or not AI is used, you bear full responsibility for the correctness and originality of what you publish; AI cannot serve as a shield for errors or plagiarism.

## 5. Column Category Suggestions

When publishing a column article, to make it easy for readers to find, it is recommended to categorize along the following directions:

- **Question walkthroughs**: detailed analysis and multiple solution methods for a particular question (especially difficult or classic ones), optionally with the question number (such as `M0001`) for easy cross-referencing.
- **Topic summaries**: systematic organization of a knowledge point, such as "Middle School Geometry Auxiliary Lines Summary" or "English Subordinate Clauses in One Chart," well suited to studying alongside a problem set.
- **Method summaries**: experience sharing on solving methods and study methods, such as "How to Check Calculation Errors" or "Where to Start on a Proof Question."
- **Study records**: personal learning journeys and exam-prep records; be careful to protect personal privacy and not disclose others' information.

The value of the discussion forum and columns comes from every user who shares seriously. One serious answer of yours may be exactly the answer to a question another student has been stuck on for three days. Please treat every word you publish to the community seriously.
:::

## 7. How to Write a Good Solution (Example Structure)

Taking a STEM proof question as an example, a well-structured solution can be organized as follows:

1. **Restate the question**: restate the question in a sentence or two and note the question number (such as `M0001`) so readers can locate it.
2. **Approach analysis**: explain what tested point it involves, why you approach it from this angle, and list the possible thinking paths along with the reasons for ruling them out.
3. **Step-by-step solution**: expand step by step, with each step corresponding to a formula or a piece of reasoning; key steps stand alone as their own paragraph, with no skipped steps.
4. **Conclusion and verification**: give the final answer and explain how to verify it (substitution, special cases, proof by contradiction, etc.).
5. **Extended thinking** (optional): variations, generalizations, and common misconceptions.

The benefit of writing this way is that readers can follow the map: first read the approach to build intuition, then check the process step by step, and finally confirm the result with the verification method. Whatever the length, keeping this skeleton means your solution will not be "a pile of formulas."

## 8. Discussion Etiquette

The atmosphere of LiuMing's discussion forum is maintained by every participant together. Beyond the content standards, please also note:

- **Be kind**: discuss the question, not the person. When pointing out an error in someone's solution, state your reasons rather than sneering.
- **Be patient**: patiently answer beginners' questions. Even when you know better, teach people to fish: explain the approach rather than just dropping the answer.
- **Be restrained**: no spamming, no posting in bursts, no bumping old threads. Before replying, check whether an identical answer already exists.
- **Be respectful**: there is no hierarchy among different solution methods; as long as one is correct, it deserves to be taken seriously. Criticize the method, not the person.

A good forum atmosphere makes more people willing to share, and more sharing in turn lets every questioner get answers faster.

## FAQ

**Q: Is asking for the answer to a homework question in the forum a violation?**
A: Asking itself is not a violation, but you should explain your own approach and where you are stuck rather than just asking for the answer. Posts that only ever ask for answers and never think are treated as low-quality content.

**Q: Can I repost solving methods from Zhihu / blogs?**
A: You can share them, but you must **mark the source and attach a link**, and ideally restate them in your own words. Copying whole passages verbatim is infringing reposting.

**Q: My article was reported; what should I do?**
A: Administrators will review the report. If the content does violate the rules, it will be handled (and can be appealed); if the report is untrue, the article is unaffected.

**Q: Can column articles be reposted elsewhere?**
A: That is up to the original author. When forwarding someone's column article, obtain their consent first and mark the source.

**Q: Is visual mode or code mode better for writing solutions?**
A: Either works. Visual mode is what-you-see-is-what-you-get and suits users unfamiliar with Markdown; code mode suits scenarios requiring precise format control. Both modes save as Markdown and can be switched at any time.
